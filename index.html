Problem Statement

The security gap in face recognition

01

Adversarial vulnerability

Tiny, crafted perturbations mislead deep learning recognition models while staying invisible to humans, enabling unauthorized access in banking, surveillance, and access control.

02

Static, fragmented defenses

Recognition, detection, and response are treated as separate parts. Static models cannot adapt to evolving or unseen attacks such as FGSM, PGD, or GAN based manipulation.

03

Lack of explainability

Models give no clear reasoning for authentication decisions, reducing trust, auditability, and administrative oversight in security sensitive settings.

Conclusion and Future Work

Toward resilient biometric security

Conclusion

VigilantFace unifies adversarial simulation, adaptive defense, risk aware decision making, and explainable AI into one cohesive cloud framework.

  • Proactive security: Red Agent stress tests with GAN, FGSM, and PGD before real threats exploit weaknesses.
  • Adaptive defense: Blue Agent classifies suspicious inputs dynamically, beyond static defenses.
  • Transparent decisions: RAG explanations keep every decision auditable and trustworthy.

Future Work

  • Autonomous red team evolution: generate novel zero day attacks via generative AI and RL.
  • Multi modal biometrics: add iris and voice factors for stronger authentication.
  • Federated learning: train across decentralized data while preserving privacy.
  • Inspector Agent: log behavior after a Blue Agent false accept (FAR).
  • Diffusion based attacks: realistic, diverse adversarial samples for robustness.
